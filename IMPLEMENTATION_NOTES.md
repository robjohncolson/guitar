# Four-bar audio Looper and Partner replies

## Pieces

`/piece.html` (`src/piece/`) adds piece learning for piano and guitar. No runtime dependency was added; CREPE (with TensorFlow.js) loads lazily on this page, only for guitar takes with plucked-note checks.

**Score.** `notation.ts` compiles the plain-text format in PIECES.md (printed or sounding pitch modes, key signatures, bar-long accidentals resolved in time order across voices on a staff, ties merged including tied accidentals across barlines, tuplets, fingering, hand overrides, pickups, repeats with endings, D.C./D.S./Fine/Coda). `midi.ts` reads MIDI as an independent check. The Gymnopédie pack was transcribed from the Mutopia LilyPond source and matches the Mutopia MIDI note for note (282 notes, staff assignments, lengths where MIDI can represent them) and the 78-bar play order.

**Course.** `analysis.ts`: signature-constrained key, authored chord names with a fit check (all Gymnopédie labels explain at least 75% of their bar's note-time), a Pardo-Birmingham style template matcher for unlabelled bars, per-hand grips, phrase-aware chunks by dynamic programming (end after long melody notes, never inside a held note), repeated material by fingerprint and a transparent difficulty ranking. `curriculum.ts` generates 114 steps for Gymnopédie, 109 to practise and five marked as repeating earlier material (21 chords, 26 changes, 10 left-hand and 11 right-hand passages, 11 hands together, 13 joins, 6 sections, the whole piece, 9 drills); `drills.ts` uses standard fingerings for all 24 scales and their arpeggios, cross-checked against three charts (a test confirms no thumb on a black key and no repeated finger in any scale). `progress.ts` implements the ladder, certification, two-day learning and spaced review; `suggestToday` makes a short session. Pedagogical defaults follow the practice research summarised for this work (Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009 on the proportion of correct trials; Killion & Duke 2025 on shrinking the task after errors; sleep-separated sessions) and are labelled as defaults, not proven optima.

**Piano listening.** `listen.ts`: log spectral flux in semitone bands in decibels (level-independent), compared with the loudest neighbouring band 15 ms earlier (SuperFlux) plus a band-breadth test, so beating between partials does not look like an attack. Each expected note is judged from its own partials (not shared with another note of the same attack, resolvable at the window length, not inside a stronger neighbour's skirt): heard needs the lowest own partial and a second low partial, including an odd one, to rise; not heard needs positive quiet (or decay after a 0.5 s gap) while the attack itself clearly sounded; neighbouring and lower candidate pitches use the same, stricter test to name wrong notes. Attack evidence is the second-largest rise of spectral peaks over the pre-attack level; between 6 and 13 dB an event is unsure and uncounted. `grade.ts` matches attacks monotonically, verifies each matched attack, sizes analysis windows by the next expected event, estimates the piano's tuning offset, and lets note checks shape the score only when at least half the checkable notes are definite. Analysis runs in a worker.

Measurement: synthetic piano tones and recorded Steinway notes (University of Iowa fixture, at 24 and 48 kHz) in Vitest; then 26 real MAESTRO v3 excerpts with aligned notes, used locally only (CC BY-NC-SA, not committed). Thresholds were tuned on the 13 development excerpts and frozen; the 13 held-out excerpts gave: nothing played credited as on time 0%; well-separated real attacks called missed 1.5%; played notes called not heard or wrong 1.8% (1.9% through a simulated laptop chain of 120 Hz high-pass, room reverb and 35 dB SNR noise); unplayed notes called heard 0.7% overall and at most 2.0% in the hardest category (a note still ringing on the pedal, not re-struck); semitone wrong notes named definitely 51%, the rest unclear. Played notes in these dense concert textures were heard 35%, unclear 43%, can't check 20%. The first rules tried were too ready to accuse (8.7% false "not heard", fixed by requiring quiet rather than a missing rise), and a bin-by-bin attack check credited 7% of silent moments (fixed by measuring rises at spectral peaks). These are concert recordings in good rooms, not the owner's piano, laptop or room.

**Guitar.** `guitar.ts`: a voicing table checked note by note against standard tuning, movable barre forms, a capo search over 0-7, a bass-and-pinch pattern that mirrors a waltz left hand, string-by-string shape exercises (CREPE), change drills, muted and fretted pattern drills, and sections and the whole piece with the melody as headphone backing. Gymnopédie: no capo, 19 shapes of tier 1-3 (Gmaj7 320002, Dmaj7 xx0222, Em9 022032, Em/D xx0000 and others), two simplifications listed for review.

**Audio and page.** `audio.ts` reuses the Looper's native worklet for capture (its beat limit was raised from 32 to 1,200 for whole-piece takes; the Looper still records 16), plus calibration, level matching and WAV export. Very slow tempos get a quiet eighth-note click. `view.ts` draws falling notes onto a keyboard (hands in two colours, fingering, verdict colours after a take, notes hidden for memory takes) and a guitar string lane with strum brackets and chord boxes.

Verification: 150 Vitest tests and a production build. `scripts/piece-smoke.mjs` in real headless Chrome: calibration through a 120 ms loopback measured 78-88 ms; a clean recorded-piano take of bars 1-4 scored 100% on time with 14 notes heard, 2 unclear and none accused; C4 in place of C sharp 4 was reported as "At bar 4, beat 2 it sounded like C4 instead of C♯4"; a silent take was all missed; a ladder take at 33 BPM recorded progress; muted guitar strums were 12/12 on time; the Em shape's six strings were all heard by the real CREPE model; one AudioContext, no page or worklet errors, no external requests, no mobile overflow. Still unverified: the owner's acoustic piano, room and microphone, and a blind measurement of PDF transcription accuracy on an unfamiliar piece.

## Guided Trainer

Trainer is the default entry at /; /play.html retains Partner and Looper. Navigation between the two Play modes dispatches the existing mode change without reloading a retained loop. Leaving for Trainer stops the old page and discards ephemeral audio. Vite builds both entry points with shared local CREPE assets. No dependencies or backend were added.

The course has 13 authored lessons across six stages, not generated scores. An attempt establishes a fixed audio-clock plan: optional demonstration, one bar of count-in, the known phrase, then recorded review. Demo voices are absent from scoring. Guidance fades from moving cues to memory to a bar-only click before tempo increases; three clean, comfortable takes are needed at each stage. Per-lesson progress is bounded and local. Comfort is supplied by the player, not inferred from their microphone. Review suggestions, a two-phrase starting-point check, creative recording, A/B take playback, looping, WAV/JSON export and a session summary complete the practice cycle.

Trainer reuses the native Looper worklet with an optional beat count (the existing default stays 16). It captures a half-second lead plus a 750 ms tail for negative and positive input offsets, then crops the retained take to its intended phrase length. Calibration uses a shorter lead. Capture chunks must arrive contiguously; dropped chunks and transport stalls make the result unscorable. One fixed, bounded gain and seam fades are used for playback/export; originals stay in memory. Stop, hidden-page events, processor errors and track loss release capture. Generation checks cover late permission, worklet events and in-flight recorded analysis. Mic scoring stops before any take playback.

Live CREPE remains available for input indication. A slow GPU can drop live queued frames, so Trainer also analyzes the completed native recording with the **same real CREPE model** before grading. This preserves original attack timestamps and gives each pluck up to four 1024-sample, 16 kHz windows at 80/140/200/260 ms after attack. At least two confident pitches must agree within half a semitone, with a 65% cluster majority. No expected note enters this analysis. Short click tails are ignored; rhythm-only grading additionally rejects coherent 1 kHz/1.3 kHz metronome tails. The native detector and quiet-release scan operate on stored audio independently of GPU speed. CREPE load failure prevents graded practice rather than substituting a detector.

The deterministic grader makes a monotonic, one-to-one timing assignment before checking pitch. Timing credit requires an error within min(130 ms, 0.16 beat); note credit allows 65 cents around the exact MIDI pitch, without octave equivalence. Extra attacks reduce timing credit; healthy silence is missed playing. Explicit damping cues are checked within 200 ms of the intended release. Their displayed tail ends in a mute mark. Uncertain pitches/releases above 15%, input clipping above 0.5% of samples, recording coverage below 90%, or interrupted timing freeze adaptation. Normal ringing gaps are not graded as rests. The interface exposes separate metrics and one next action rather than collapsing all problems into an unexplained score.

Calibration detects at least six of eight known 1 kHz pips, requires less than 35 ms spread, and measures the round trip minus reported output latency. It never fits an offset to the player's attacks. Stored calibration is tied to input device ID, native sample rate and input processing; device-change events invalidate it. The reported outputLatency is not part of the identity key because it can fluctuate between resumes. A manual -300 to +500 ms offset is explicit. Hardware/driver delays, Bluetooth jitter, reverberation and changing room placement remain physical uncertainties; recalibrate after a path change. A stable loopback simulation is not a claim of exact acoustic timing on every device.

Borrowed structure: aim-dojo's establish/sample/hold rhythm practice and thinning external cues informed the between-attempt support sequence. Tempo stays fixed within a phrase; no guitar beat tracking or transfer of aim-dojo's runtime tempo formulas. Pedagogical scope was checked against [Berklee Guitar for Beginners](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/guitar-for-beginners), [JustinGuitar's beginner course](https://www.justinguitar.com/beginner) and [metronome introduction](https://www.justinguitar.com/guitar-lessons/introducing-the-metronome-bc-125). Lesson phrases and copy here are authored for this app. [CREPE](https://github.com/marl/crepe) is monophonic: scored string changes need the previous string quieted; full chord shapes, picking direction and physical technique are guidance/self-review, not microphone-certified skills.

Phase gates passed in order: 112 tests for curriculum/grading/progress; 117 after transport/calibration; 117 plus the first multi-entry build; then 124 tests after recorded analysis, cancellation and click-tail regressions. Chrome verified nine clean takes progressing 60 BPM from guided to memory to sparse click, certifying 60 and proposing 62; wrong/late/extra/silent/noisy inputs had the expected distinct outcomes (15 scored traces). A second complete Chrome run covered all 13 lesson patterns, long-note rest failures, creative capture, A/B playback, loop review, permission cancellation, mobile layout and reload with a simulated 350 ms output path (measured offset -214 ms). All 13 clean patterns received full credit; uncertain noise remained unscored. The native Looper regression also passed on the new Play route, including header navigation without losing the take; independent output comparison error stayed below 3e-8 at a matched peak of 0.8. Desktop/mobile layouts were inspected and the lane was redrawn at device width for legible phone frets.

Automated inputs use the existing attributed guitar recording, transposed to independently authored test patterns, with native Web Audio capture, actual worklets and the actual vendored model. Noise bursts stand in for muted strums. The final local quick run also passed the two-phrase starting-point check (suggesting the phrase lesson after clear pulse and crossing takes) and Stop during active capture. A human acoustic-guitar/headphone/room session is still needed to judge feel and technique guidance.


## Automatic Looper level matching

Previously, 100% loop volume applied unity gain to a raw microphone recording, so a quiet take stayed quiet. The native capture engine now tracks the absolute sample peak incrementally and freezes `min(16, 0.8 / peak)` as `levelGain` at completion. Peaks below 0.005 remain at unity. This is static peak normalization, preserving dynamics without compressor pumping, waveform shaping, a second scan at completion, or added playback latency. Finite input samples remain unchanged; invalid nonfinite samples become silence.

Each take carries its own gain through completion, Replace, Undo, Stop/Play and Stop-completion rollback. Playback applies that gain before the existing seam fade, optional headphone softening and user volume. At 100% volume the loop sample peak stays at or below 0.8, leaving room for the metronome's 0.126 maximum amplitude. This is a sample-peak bound, not a claim to repair microphone clipping or a calibrated true-peak mastering process. The UI shows the rounded gain in dB and draws the waveform at its matched level. WAV export applies the same fixed gain and edge fades independently of monitoring controls; raw samples remain intact in memory.

Phase 1 passed all 99 tests, including quiet, hot and nearly silent level calculations. Phase 2 passed all 101 tests and the production build: a late peak controls the first playback pass, accents and rests survive, distinct take gains follow Undo, native softening still works, and export preserves normalized PCM. The quiet recorded-guitar Chrome check passed with a 0.06495 input peak raised by 12.318× (about +22 dB) to a 0.8 playback peak at 100% volume. Two passes and Undo matched the independently calculated waveform within 2.99e-8; WAV differences stayed within half a 16-bit PCM step. Replace, cancellation, Stop/Play at 50%, unchanged WAV after Undo/volume changes, mute, mic races, mode switching, reload, and mobile overflow passed with no browser or worklet errors. The display-only waveform adjustment was followed by all 101 tests and another successful build.

The broader real-CREPE Partner browser check was not clean in this session: the updated build recognized a two-note second phrase and produced 15 rather than the fixture's expected 18 replies; a comparison against the still-unmodified live deployment failed the late-note assertion. Both loaded and ran real CREPE without browser exceptions. These are different failures and do not establish identical causes; Partner detection/scheduling remains variable. No Partner pitch, phrase, masking or scheduling implementation changed for this gain revision, and its unit regressions passed. The historical successful Partner run below predates this revision.

## Looper addition

The user-approved Looper is a separate app mode. It records native-rate mono audio for 16 beats in 4/4, after a four-beat count-in, and plays it continuously. The existing Partner still runs real CREPE and retains its finite Echo/Harmony behavior. No new model, dependency, backend, upload, or alternative pitch detector was added.

`loop-engine.ts` implements sample-boundary recording, continuous playback, replacement, Undo, cancellation, and 3 ms playback edge fades. `looper.worklet.ts` owns the actual audio buffers and output. It anchors its frame counter to the AudioContext sample clock and increments by rendered block length; occasional repeated `currentFrame` readings observed in the Chrome check cannot overwrite a block or leave a hole in a recording. A regression exercises this case. This remains an audio render clock, independent of guitar tempo or main-thread timers.

`AudioLooper` acquires the microphone before arming a future recording boundary. First recording, or recording while stopped, gets one bar of count-in. Replacement while playing starts at the next loop boundary; old playback stops during the four-bar recording. At completion, the worklet immediately starts its new buffer. Native-rate audio reaches the UI in chunks of at most 4096 samples for the waveform and WAV export, avoiding a full-take clone on the audio thread. At 40 BPM a take is 24 seconds, beyond the diagnostic recorder's old 20-second cap.

Completed and previous takes stay in page memory. Stop cancels an incomplete replacement, releases active or late-granted microphone streams, and keeps the last take. A small rollback snapshot reconciles Stop arriving just before the UI receives a completed-take or Undo event, preserving agreement between saved audio and worklet playback. Undo switches on a loop boundary during playback and stays silent when stopped. Clear and reload discard audio; changing app mode stops both transports while retaining the Looper take.

Looper metronome clicks use the existing synth with beat-one accents, independently saved click/mute preferences, and scheduling against AudioContext time minus outputLatency. The worklet's rounded native-frame loop length also determines click spacing to avoid cumulative drift. BPM and processing changes cannot interrupt recording; BPM stays fixed while playing. After Stop, a new BPM applies to Record, while Play restores the old take's recorded BPM. Undo restores its take's tempo.

Optional headphone softening starts off. Live RMS above 0.012 moves output toward 55% over an 80 ms time constant; quiet recovers over 350 ms. It operates on playback gain, not stored samples, and uses no pitch gate. Speakers disables this feature. Without softening, the microphone closes after recording; with it, the mic listens during playback until Stop. WAV export uses the native sample rate, mono 16-bit PCM, and the same tiny edge fades as playback.

The recording and playback boundaries share the compensated audio render clock. Physical input latency is not automatically calibrated; acoustic latency, room decay, and speaker/metronome bleed still require a real guitar/room check. Headphones are recommended for clean raw audio. CREPE pitch masking remains active in Partner but cannot remove sound from raw Looper recordings.

Looper phase gates: 89 tests after the sample engine; 92 after native audio/microphone transport; 94 and a production build after UI/preferences/export. Subsequent Stop-completion and repeated-frame/softening regressions bring the suite to 96 tests. The separate Chrome Looper smoke uses overlapping real guitar plucks and an independent output-probe worklet, checking actual recorded/output sample values and four-bar timing rather than oscillator scheduling alone.

The completed local Chrome check captured 384,000 samples at 48 kHz for four bars at 120 BPM. Two complete playback passes, the restored Undo take, and Stop/Play reproduced the saved waveform within the 0.00001 sample tolerance (maximum observed difference approximately 8.35e-9 after gain and edge fades). Replacement output was silent for exactly the recording window. Count-in accents, BPM locking/restoration, cancellation, mute, mode switching, microphone permission races, native-rate WAV size, page-only persistence, mobile overflow and a single AudioContext passed, with no browser/processor errors or external HTTP runtime requests. Test-only external audio was muted; software WebGL loaded the retained real CREPE model. Physical speaker feedback and microphone latency were not established by this automated check.

The Partner regression after Looper integration also passed with real CREPE and recorded guitar: 29/29 plucks detected (27 accepted, two correctly masked), 18 finite reply notes, fixed Harmony followed by Echo, no overlapping voices or late scheduling, and no browser errors. Its Stop, reset, microphone changes, preferences, recording inspection, and mobile checks passed. No pitch-ear implementation or Partner musical rule was changed for Looper.

## Partner

## Harmony

`src/music/harmony.ts` makes a deterministic decision from the complete source loop. It considers twelve diatonic collections, with major and relative-minor triad evidence and modest first/last tonic preferences. It requires at least three distinct pitch classes. A complete triad with a scoring margin, a unique compatible collection with four or more notes, or agreement on all proposed thirds supports a harmony; otherwise it selects an octave reply. It does not identify strummed chords or claim to infer a unique key.

For each source note, candidates favor a diatonic third, with sixth, fifth, and octave alternatives in MIDI 48–84. Costs include register, distance above the source, and overlap with predicted guitar notes during the actual half-beat-delayed reply span. Known rests contribute no collision cost; unknown lengths conservatively fill the available source space. A closed-path dynamic program minimizes jumps within the line and across its repeated boundary. The resulting line uses median source detuning. The octave fallback preserves each source pitch and tuning, moved into the supported register by octaves.

`PhraseReply` freezes the selected pitches when an exchange starts, leaving all source offsets, durations, cycle length, and scheduling intact. Harmony uses 72% of the source velocity; an ambiguous octave reply uses 55%. The third pass uses a further 80% factor. Echo preserves its existing pitch/tuning and velocity. No chord voices or extra notes are added.

The mode selector persists and affects only the next exchange. The current line cannot change mid-reply, and a waiting loop uses the latest selection when it starts. UI text identifies a pending change or the soft octave fallback. Recognition/deduplication retain the original player motif; self-masking receives actual synthesized pitches and octaves. Diagnostic reply events include `kind` so changing the selector cannot relabel earlier output.

## Ear and rests

`src/audio/attack.ts` uses rolling 12 ms energy and high-frequency-difference windows, a 40 ms reference envelope, and 90 ms debounce. This avoids treating individual waveform cycles or ringing modulation as separate plucks. `onset.ts` waits at least 60 ms for two agreeing pitch observations with 65% support within 0.65 semitones, rejecting unsettled pitch after 140 ms. Raw attacks establish timestamps; pitch jumps alone do not create notes.

Note ends come from 80 ms of quiet raw audio (below the larger of 0.0015 RMS and 4% of the accepted attack peak), timestamped at the beginning of quiet, or the next accepted attack. All captured frames supply energy independently of GPU inference, so skipped pitch frames cannot hide a short pause. Confidence dropouts and octave blips in sustained audio do not invent rests. Self-masking occurs before a candidate can end a player's sustain. The observed end still has frame-window and room-decay uncertainty; it is not a precise finger-release transcription.

CREPE remains the actual pitch ear: local tiny tfjs Layers model, WebGL, official [1,1024] normalization and 360-bin postprocessing, AudioWorklet capture at 16 kHz with 20 ms hops. Inference keeps snapshots at least 40 and 80 ms after each raw attack, at most four total, plus the latest frame; snapshots older than 500 ms are dropped. This avoids relying on the unstable initial transient or losing the second observation on a busy GPU. Raw attacks are delivered in inferred-frame order, so a new live pluck cannot overwrite one still being analyzed. Two seconds of confirmed release timestamps preserve note ends when inference finishes after the next pluck. YIN is available only on model-load failure and is labeled `FALLBACK: YIN`.

## Loop recognition

`src/music/motif.ts` keeps at most 96 accepted attacks within 64 entered beats. Repeated pitch-class lags propose cycles of 1–16 beats; adjacent lags within 0.12 entered beats form median estimates. Recognition advances with the audio clock even in silence, waiting for the third full cycle including the closing rest.

Three cycle windows are aligned by pitch, relative attack time, and known note duration. Phrases contain 2–24 notes and at least two pitch classes. Alignment permits one insertion/deletion per comparison for phrases of three or more notes; a wrong pitch can use that same allowance at five or more notes. Timing tolerance is min(85 ms, 0.18 entered beats) plus 3.5% of elapsed phrase time. Known duration differences must fit max(90 ms, 0.22 beats, 30% of the shorter duration).

At least two performances must agree on every retained note. Consensus uses median pitch/tuning, timing, known lengths, and dynamics, discarding isolated extras. Gaps after known note ends, including the final gap, form explicit rests. Unknown ends remain uncertain and playback fills available note space instead of inventing a short note/rest. A following accepted attack clips an earlier duration before matching. For repeat suppression, rotated pitch/rhythm patterns, articulation changes, and a one-note dropout at the same phase count as the same idea. This favors quiet over re-answering a fragment; L permits intentional variations. After recognition, only input at the next cycle boundary is retained, allowing immediate new ideas without recycling old evidence.

## Playback and room handling

`reply.ts` schedules exactly three captured cycles. `companion.ts` chooses the first schedulable occurrence of the loop's own phase plus half an entered beat; it does not round to bars. A waiting loop starts after the current full three-cycle window using its own phase. Relative timings and measured cycle duration are preserved even for a new idea at a different pace. One waiting idea may be replaced by a newer different one; no backlog or indefinite retrigger.

Audio events use AudioContext time minus `outputLatency || baseLatency || 0`, with 75 ms lookahead and 15 ms late tolerance. Slightly late notes are shortened; stale notes are skipped without catch-up. Note lengths account for the oscillator stop tail and leave a gap before the next app voice. Muting does not extend active passes; a waiting phrase waits for unmute. L fades current voices and resets loops while preserving pulse. Stop is immediate; stopped masks retain only the brief audible tail.

Reply notes and adjacent octaves are masked through their audible duration plus 80 ms. Recent windows remain available for one second afterward to check delayed inference against its original timestamp; this does not extend their audible masking interval. Speakers mode also requests AEC. Matching real guitar attacks can be masked, and room sound can obscure silence, so physical feedback remains a limitation. Only accepted attacks affect touch calibration and optional responsive softening. No model, backend, or runtime API was added.

## Optional inspection

`listening-capture.ts` allocates a maximum 20-second, 16 kHz local buffer only after an explicit button press. Timestamped overlapping frames reconstruct a WAV; JSON records raw attacks, pitch/RMS observations, accepted/masked notes and durations, and scheduled replies. Late inference annotations refresh the trace after recording stops. The waveform and note bars let the player compare actual sound with detections. Clip playback stops the live microphone. Audio and notes are neither persisted nor uploaded; only preferences use localStorage. Obsolete Auto settings are discarded.
