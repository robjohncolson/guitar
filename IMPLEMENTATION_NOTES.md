# Repeater and phrase memory implementation

The expressive revision preserves attack strength and note length, retains the last useful phrase through silence, and adds Responsive on/off (default on). `Touch` maps unnormalized attack-window median RMS against the previous 24 accepted player levels, with a square-root curve and bounded velocity. Self-masked notes do not influence calibration. The synth scales the envelope and filter brightness with velocity.

The onset detector reports releases after 80 ms continuously unvoiced, timestamped at the beginning of that interval, or at the next settled attack. Lengths are estimates of monophonic articulation, not a recording of the guitar envelope. A queued ordinary repeat uses the duration known at scheduling time, capped at two beats; releases arriving after scheduling do not reshape that tone. Frozen memory clips lengths at the next note or source boundary. The matching-pitch mask covers the full synthesized duration plus 80 ms.

Phrase memory adds one 1/2/4-bar selector and Remember/Clear (L), defaulting to 2 bars. `src/music/phrase-memory.ts` keeps bounded rolling player history, one candidate per memory length, and a separate immutable playback snapshot. Completed bars with new playing refresh candidates; silence leaves their note offsets and rests intact. Late-settling attacks update a candidate until it is frozen. Playback starts at the next schedulable downbeat, preserving fractional beat offsets, exact detected Hz, velocity, duration, and rests. New playing updates only rolling history and candidates. The snapshot never overdubs, and ordinary echoes are suppressed until Clear. Both modes share the existing synth and self-mask. Empty or incomplete listening captures are unavailable.

`Response` accumulates only accepted player attacks. Each adds 0.3 activity, capped at 1, with a 0.75-beat hold and 1.25-beat exponential recovery. The loop gain target is `1 - 0.6 * activity`; a dedicated gain stage follows it with 40 ms softening and 160 ms return time constants using Web Audio automation. Current and future tones respond together. Master mute/volume and click are separate, so recovery cannot undo a mute. The scheduler bypasses response outside memory mode. The toggle preserves the frozen phrase and is saved in settings.

Loop events are generated by cycle and note index against the audio clock; there is no accumulating playback timer or queue. A delayed scheduler jumps past missed cycles instead of emitting a burst. Muting keeps the phrase and phase, Clear/Panic stops current and queued loop tones, and BPM/pulse changes reset history and loop together. `memoryBars` and `responsive` are persisted alongside existing preferences, defaulting older saved preferences to 2 bars and Responsive on; note data never persists. The memory-specific tests cover 1/2/4-bar capture, bar boundaries, rests and off-grid notes, fixed snapshots, repeat timing, latency, clipping at the phrase end, mute/resume, Clear/reset, and self-mask integration.

The user's simplified product replaces scale inference, lock, six-onset gating, four roles, density, and hole-filling. Those modules and tests were removed. The first accepted attack queues a single repeat of its detected Hz at `onset.t + 120 / bpm`; fractional MIDI preserves tuning. Notes are not quantized and occupancy never suppresses a repeat.

Direct BPM entry (40–240, default 100) starts the pulse when audio is enabled. Space tapping remains optional. BPM now persists alongside microphone/output preferences. A BPM change or Shift+Space clears queued/current repeats. M clears repeats without affecting click; Escape clears both while keeping the pulse. No pending echo survives muting and later unmuting.

`src/music/repeater.ts` owns the queue and self-mask. `src/audio/scheduler.ts` schedules repeats at their target time minus `outputLatency || baseLatency || 0`, with 75 ms lookahead and the existing 15 ms late tolerance. The 60 ms pitch-settling period does not add to the delay because events retain the original attack timestamp. Stalled-tab events are dropped rather than replayed in a burst.

The pitch ear remains real local CREPE tiny, tfjs WebGL, official [1,1024] normalization and 360-bin postprocessing, AudioWorklet capture with 20 ms frame hops, and attack-based segmentation. Raw RMS is retained before normalization for touch; it never claims occupancy or drives responsive volume. Speakers mode also uses AEC. A matching real guitar attack during a self-mask window can be indistinguishable from speaker bleed. Headphones is the escape hatch. Physical guitar/speaker acceptance is still a human check.

Model source: `https://marl.github.io/crepe/model/model.json`, with the 13 `group*-shard1of1` paths relative to that directory (1,948,384 weight bytes). Tiny only. `npm run vendor:crepe` preserves the upstream model and licenses and records hashes. The build verifies every model asset. CREPE MIT license: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marl/crepe/master/LICENSE`; TensorFlow.js Apache license: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tensorflow/tfjs/master/LICENSE`.

The browser check measures synthesized pitch, envelope peaks, note length and schedule timing, holds a candidate through disabled microphone input, compares frozen cycles, checks response recovery and its saved toggle, and exercises mute, Clear, panic, and reload. No physical guitar or room acoustics are simulated by that check.

Human acceptance: open the live app in Chrome, enter 120 BPM, enable audio/mic, wait for CREPE ready, and pluck one string. Hear that pitch once a second later without any lock or training notes. Try several different notes, including between beats, then stop and ensure repeats stop. Set 80 BPM and expect 1.5 seconds; use M or Escape before a queued repeat and confirm it never sounds. Test laptop speakers for recursive feedback and H/headphones if needed.

For expressive memory, alternate quiet/loud and damped/ringing notes over 1, 2, or 4 bars. Pause several bars, then Remember: the useful phrase should still be available with its original accents and rests. Improvise a busy line over it and then pause; the loop should soften and return smoothly. Compare Responsive off, confirm click remains steady, and use M to confirm recovery does not unmute the loop.
